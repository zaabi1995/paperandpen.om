The U.S. International Trade Administration describes a proforma invoice as a quote in invoice format. A buyer may use it to arrange an import licence, pre-shipment inspection, a letter of credit or access to the currency needed for an overseas purchase. That makes it useful before the sale is final, but it does not replace the documents required once the transaction happens.

## Proforma invoice meaning

A proforma invoice is a **detailed proposal for a future sale**. It looks like an invoice because it lists the buyer, seller, items, values and commercial terms, but it is issued before the goods or services are supplied. Its job is to give the buyer enough confirmed detail to approve the purchase, arrange payment or prepare import paperwork.

Do not treat the word "proforma" as a universal legal category. Tax points, advance payments and invoice requirements differ by country. For Oman, check the [source-linked Oman VAT guide](/vat/oman/) and the Tax Authority regulations before relying on a document for VAT purposes.

## When should you use a proforma invoice?

Use one when the buyer needs invoice-level detail before the final sale document can be issued. Common situations include:

- **International trade:** the buyer needs values, weights, delivery terms or an estimated shipping date for import approval.
- **Internal purchase approval:** a finance or procurement team needs a formal document before releasing a budget or purchase order.
- **Advance-payment arrangements:** both parties want the proposed items, currency and payment terms recorded before money moves.
- **Custom work:** the scope is agreed closely enough for an itemised proposal, but the work or delivery has not happened yet.

If the buyer only needs an early price offer, a [quotation](/invoicing/quotations/) may be enough. The guide to [quotations, estimates and proforma invoices](/blog/quotation-vs-estimate-vs-proforma-invoice/) explains where each document fits.

## A simple proforma invoice example

Imagine a Muscat equipment supplier preparing an overseas order. The buyer needs a document for import approval before the supplier ships anything. The supplier issues a proforma invoice showing both parties, the equipment descriptions and quantities, shipment weights, the currency, agreed Incoterm, payment terms, estimated shipping date and the date until which the proposal remains valid.

The buyer uses that document to complete approval and import arrangements. If the order proceeds, the seller then issues the commercial, tax and payment documents required for the actual transaction. The proforma remains part of the negotiation record, not a substitute for the final invoice.
